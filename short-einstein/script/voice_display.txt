The man who rewrote the universe couldn't even land a university job.

Imagine you're his boss. 1905, a Swiss patent office. Your clerk Albert checks inventions all day. Clocks. Typewriters. More clocks.

Then, in one year, he publishes four papers that flip physics upside down.

One proves atoms are real. One shows light comes in tiny packets, and later wins him the Nobel Prize. One gives us E=mc².

And the wildest: time isn't the same for everyone. Move fast enough and your clock ticks slower. How is that possible?

Picture a bowling ball on a trampoline. Ten years later, Einstein says gravity is just that: mass bending space and time.

In 1919, an eclipse proved starlight really bends around the sun. Overnight, the clerk became the most famous scientist alive.

Today, every GPS satellite runs his math. Skip it, and your map drifts about 10 kilometers a day.

Not bad for a guy who checked clocks for a living.
