## 2. What If You Had 500 IQ? — Ifverse (~8:56)
https://youtu.be/4kHPfx6Gy00

**[0:00] ХУК**
Your phone battery lasts a month. You rebuilt it at breakfast. One new chip, one bent wire. Full signal in an elevator. Three floors underground. Phone companies would pay millions for that. And your tea is still hot.

**[0:17] ЗАГОЛОВОК + НАУКА**
What if you had 500 IQ? Most people score around 100 on an IQ test. Above 140 is rare. And most tests can't even measure past about 160. You? 500.
"When did you put that there?" "Last Tuesday. The new part comes today."
Your body is totally normal, but your mind sees the answer before anyone else sees the problem.

**[0:47] ПЛЮСЫ / МИНУСЫ**
So, what's life like with 500? Amazing and awful. Usually both at once.
Plus, there are no hard puzzles left. Your sister's Olympiad problem? A crossword. "I've been stuck on that for three days!" "Lucky guess."
Minus. Nothing excites you. Deep in your brain, dopamine cells fire hardest for good surprises. But you expect everything, so the spark never comes. "Wow, socks." You know every punchline in advance, so you laugh a beat late, on purpose.
You invent things just for you. A mug that keeps tea at exactly 60 degrees Celsius. A lock that knows your walk. A wardrobe that folds your shirts. "Leo, your door hates me!" "You walk like you're late." "I am late!"
The catch? You can never show the world. Show one mug, and tomorrow companies are lining up outside your door. So everything you build stays home.
You see the logic behind everything people do. So you pick the longest line and get out first. Which is why people make no sense to you. They fight in lines. They pull doors that say push. They make the same mistake ten times.
Imagine a sane man locked in an asylum for years. He copies everyone to fit in until he forgets he's pretending. Every day you play dumb to look normal. And deep down, you're scared that one day it won't be an act.
You see the weak spot in any system. "Is my school's website safe?" "Change your password." "Today?" "Now." To you, every system is an open door. You only ever opened one. Your own.
So you can't trust anything anymore. Everyone thinks the world is solid. Only you know how fragile it is.
You could beat the stock market. Or crack a bank. Millionaire by next week. But every extra dollar you win, someone else loses. And you're not a thief. Besides, money brings attention.
"If you're so smart, why aren't you rich?" "Because I'm smart." So you chose a quiet life. Invisible.
Your test scores? Always 100. Exactly average. For you, that's the hard part. Your data? Quietly wiped from every list that sells people. Only yours. Nobody's looking for you. That's the real superpower.
"My Leo? He's an engineer. A good, normal boy." The price? Nobody knows who you really are. Not even your mom.

**[3:56] ВТОРОЙ АКТ: ЭМОЦИОНАЛЬНАЯ ЛИНИЯ (МАМА)**
So the smartest person alive lived the most ordinary life. And you'd have died of boredom if not for one person. Your mom.
Since the day Mia was born, she worked two jobs. And every month she put a little aside. One account, one dream. Mia's university. She walked home to save the bus fare. She always gave you the last slice. She kept every drawing Mia ever made.
None of it made sense. Other people's nonsense drove you crazy. Hers, you loved.
"Surprise party tonight! Don't tell her anything." "I never tell anyone anything."

**[4:48] КОНФЛИКТ: МОШЕННИКИ**
"Good afternoon. This is your bank's fraud team. Someone is emptying your account. Right now." "What? No, no, that's my daughter's money." "Don't worry. We'll move it to a safe account. Stay on the line. Don't call anyone."
Real banks never ask you to move money. But fear makes people fast. And he never let her hang up. You were at work across town. Even you can't predict what you can't see.
"Don't. Don't turn on the light. I'm so stupid. 17 years of Mia's money, and I just gave it away. Please, don't tell Mia."
"Make a wish, Mom." "I wish I could take today back."
You could steal it all back in a minute. Ruin them all. But then you'd be just like them.
"You're not stupid, Mom. They're good at lies. I'm better at the truth."

**[6:01] РАСПЛАТА**
For the first time, you opened someone else's door. The number was fake. The account was real. You pulled that thread.
Four minutes later, a basement on the edge of town. 40 phones. And a list. 300 names. Almost all of them old. One note said "pension, last savings, paid." And at the bottom, your mother, with a smiley face.
"I love old people. They're polite. They trust a kind voice. And they say thank you while you rob them. A whole university fund. Her birthday. My present."
You didn't take a cent. You just showed everyone the truth. Every bank got the evidence. Every account in the chain froze. The police had hunted this gang for a year. Tonight they got an address.
And on 40 screens, one message. "Hello? You called my mother today." "Who is this?" "Her son. You should get the door."

**[7:23] КАТАРСИС**
"It's back, all of it." "What's back?" "Nothing. Everything." "Come here!"
That morning 300 phones lit up across the country. 300 people got their savings back. And a little faith in people too.
You'd predicted every step. The arrests, the refunds. But not this. 300 strangers laughing and crying at the same moment. Something better than you expected. And the spark finally lit up.
"Next year for my birthday, no phone calls please."
And for the first time in your life, you laughed right on time.

**[8:15] КЛИФФХЭНГЕР + CTA**
That night your computer turned on by itself. And someone started typing. "Nice work, Leo. That call center was one of ours. Nobody is that invisible by accident. We've been looking for you for years."
Your whole life, nobody had ever been one step ahead of you. Until tonight. Finally, a real puzzle.

## 3. What If You Could See Everyone's Future Wealth? — ConChaser (~10:48)
https://youtu.be/DK4EOHCrVvM

**[0:00] ХУК**
"Sir, you dropped this." You bend down to pick up a $5 bill. The homeless man turns around, and that's when you see it, floating above his head. $8,742,619,300. Eight billion dollars.
You blink. Look at his ripped shoes, his cardboard sign, the half-eaten sandwich sitting beside him. Then, back at the number. Eight billion dollars.
You check your own head in a shop window. $43,217. Great. Apparently, the homeless guy is going to be richer than you by approximately $8 billion.
But here's the problem. You have absolutely no idea what these numbers mean. And by the end of the week, you're going to wish you never found out.

**[0:45] ПРЕДЫСТОРИЯ: УТРО**
Because this morning, something very strange happened. You woke up, brushed your teeth and walked into the kitchen. Your mom was making breakfast. Nothing unusual, except for the giant glowing number floating above her head. $286,400. You rubbed your eyes. Still there. You looked at your dad. $91,200. Fair enough.
You grab your phone, search for symptoms. "Why am I seeing floating numbers above people's heads?" Google suggests you might need more sleep. Thanks, Google. Very helpful.

**[1:20] ПРАВИЛА СИЛЫ**
But when you walk outside, things get even weirder. Every single person has a number. The mailman, $312,000. The woman jogging past you, $1.4 million. The guy yelling at a parking meter, $27. You actually feel a little bad for him.
Then you notice something interesting. The numbers aren't showing how much money people have right now. Your neighbor drives a Lamborghini. His number, $0. Meanwhile, the teenager delivering newspapers has $16 million floating above his head.
And suddenly, it clicks. You're not seeing people's current wealth, you're seeing their future wealth. Specifically, the highest net worth they'll ever reach.
And if that's true, you might have just discovered the most valuable superpower in human history. Naturally, your first thought is to use it responsibly. By which you mean, getting extremely rich.

**[2:12] ПОПЫТКА №1: УЗНАТЬ СЕКРЕТ**
You head straight to the nearest shopping mall. You walk past a businessman wearing a $10,000 watch. $140,000. Interesting. A janitor pushing a cleaning cart? $3.2 million. Okay, now we're talking.
You follow him. Not suspiciously, just a completely normal person following a janitor through a shopping mall. "Excuse me, do you invest in anything?" "Uh, no." "Crypto?" "No." "Real estate?" "No." "Are you secretly related to a billionaire?" "Kid, I'm trying to clean the floor." Right. Good conversation.
You spot a little girl selling homemade bracelets outside the mall. Her number? $92 million. You buy a bracelet. It costs $3. "So, what's your business strategy?" She's seven. She says she likes purple. Excellent. Your financial research is going incredibly well.

**[3:13] ПОПЫТКА №2: СТАВКА НА БУДУЩЕГО МИЛЛИАРДЕРА**
What if you just need to find someone who will get rich? You spend three days walking around the city, looking for the biggest number.
A college student sitting alone in a coffee shop. Messy hair, old laptop. Above his head, $64,800,000,000. $64 billion. That's more than most famous billionaires.
"Hey, what are you working on?" "Just an app." Of course, it's always an app. "Something that helps people organize their lives." Extremely vague, but very interesting.
He talks for 40 minutes. You understand approximately 12% of it, but you understand the number above his head perfectly. "Actually, yeah, I could use someone to help with marketing." And just like that, you're in.
A month building the company. Every morning, you check his number. Still $64 billion. Perfect.

**[4:26] ТВИСТ №1: ЦИФРЫ — ЭТО ПРОГНОЗ**
An investor offers $2 million for a piece of the company. His number is $18 million. Your friend's is $64.8 billion. So you whisper, "Don't take the deal." Your friend rejects the offer. Or so you think you saved him.
Because the number changes. $64.8 billion becomes $4.2 million. These numbers aren't guaranteed. They're predictions. And your actions can change them. You didn't save your friend's company, you may have just destroyed it.
If one tiny decision can erase $60 billion, what else can you change?
The investor was connected to a major tech company. "I trusted you." You're out. Your own number: $43,217 → $12,600. You've somehow managed to make both of you poorer.

**[6:05] ГЛАВНАЯ ЗАГАДКА: БЕЗДОМНЫЙ**
If the numbers can change, why did that homeless man still have $8 billion? "What's your name?" "Daniel." Construction worker, injured, lost his job, couldn't pay rent. A daughter. "She thinks I abandoned her. I've been trying to find her." And suddenly, you stop thinking about money.
You buy Daniel a meal, help him find a place to stay, spend two days searching for his daughter. She's 26, works at a small medical research company. Above her head — $8,742,619,300. The exact same number. Down to the dollar.

**[7:45] ТВИСТ №2 + КАТАРСИС**
They meet. She cries. They hug. And Daniel's number changes: $8 billion → $86,000.
The number shows the highest wealth along their current path. Without him she was going to quit her research. Daniel's original number reflected a future where he would inherit her fortune after her death. Now they have each other. He has no idea what he's lost, and he couldn't look happier.

**[9:08] ТВИСТ №3: $0 = ВРЕМЯ**
A young man in an expensive suit. $0. A delivery truck, a driver looking at his phone. The number isn't just about money, it's also about time. "Look out!" The truck misses him by inches. $0 → $2.8 million. You just saved his life.

**[9:59] ФИНАЛ + CTA**
Every number is a future that hasn't happened yet. You still have $12,600. But your number starts moving. $12,601... $12,602... One dollar at a time. Maybe for the first time in your life, you're investing in something that matters. People.
If you could see exactly how rich everyone would become, would you use it to make yourself wealthy? Or would you change someone's future, even if it meant losing your own fortune?

## 4. Can 2 People Really Restart Humanity? — Awaken (~6:58)
https://youtu.be/6oZ9fdM0n50

**[0:00] ХУК**
You wake up on a Monday and something is wrong. No traffic, no sirens, no neighbor drilling into the wall at 7:00 AM. Overnight, every person on Earth has vanished. Well, almost every person. "So, it's just us?" Which means the entire future of the human species now rests on two strangers who met about 40 seconds ago.

**[0:29] ВОПРОС**
Could two people actually repopulate Earth? Seriously?

**[0:38] МАТЕМАТИКА («ЛЕГКО»)**
First, the target. A little over 8 billion people. The good news is humans can have a lot of children. In the early 1900s, married Hutterite women in North America averaged around 10 kids each — about as close to the biological limit as any population has ever been recorded. The record? A Russian peasant woman in the 1700s reportedly had 69. Reportedly. Nobody has ever been able to check.
Say every couple raises eight kids. Each generation is four times bigger. Two become eight, eight becomes 32, 32 becomes 128. You pass 8 billion in about 16 generations. Roughly 400 years. Easy.

**[1:31] ПРОБЛЕМА**
Except, there's a problem with generation two. Year one, a crib. Year five, a bunk bed. Year 12, a house so loud you start sleeping in the car. 20 years in, your kids want families of their own. So they look around at the only other young people on the planet. Their brothers and sisters. Oh no.

**[1:59] НАУКА: ГЕНЕТИКА**
Your genes come in pairs. If one copy is broken, the other usually covers for it, like a spare tire. The average person quietly carries one or two mutations that would be deadly to a child who inherited two copies. A random partner rarely shares the same broken gene; siblings often do. In children of the closest relatives, about three in 10 died young or had a severe disability.

**[2:51] ИСТОРИЯ: ГАБСБУРГИ**
Spain's Habsburg kings married their relatives again and again. King Charles II's inbreeding was about the same as the child of a brother and sister. Sick most of his life, never had children. When he died in 1700, the dynasty died with him.

**[3:16] УХУДШЕНИЕ**
Your grandkids are cousins. Their kids are cousins twice over. Every new baby arrives with around 60 brand-new mutations. Between the two of you, at most four versions of any gene. That's the entire toolbox for the future of the human race. A sickness that finds a weak spot in one of you might find it in all of you.
Decades later, on the porch, you wonder whether you gave them a future or just a very slow ending.

**[4:20] НАДЕЖДА: РЕАЛЬНЫЕ ПРИМЕРЫ**
In 1980, the black robin of the Chatham Islands was down to five birds. One breeding pair, Old Blue and Old Yellow. Conservationists slipped her eggs into other nests so she would lay again. Every black robin alive today is her descendant.
Northern elephant seals: about 20 in the 1890s, more than 200,000 today.
Pitcairn Island, 1790: 27 people. 66 years later, 193 descendants had to be moved.
Around 900,000 years ago our ancestors crashed to roughly 1,280 breeding adults. If that's right, all 8 billion of us came from a group that could fit inside a high school gym.

**[5:48] ОТВЕТ**
Technically, maybe. But every species that squeezed through a gap this small paid a price. And we only ever hear about the ones that made it. Simulations of a generation ship: the smallest crew that avoided serious inbreeding was 98 people. 49 times more than you've got. We should've made more friends.

**[6:18] CTA**
If it all came down to you and one other person, who would you want standing next to you on that empty street? ("WHO'S YOUR +1?")
