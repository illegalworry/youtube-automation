## [0:00] HOOK

8:59. Your alarm didn't go off. Your exam starts at 9:00, on the other side of the city. Forty minutes by bus. You're wearing one sock.

You squeeze your eyes shut, picture the school hallway, and...

Pop.

Grey floor. Buzzing lights. Locker 214. You're standing in the hallway. Still holding your toothbrush.

The janitor drops his mop. "Where... where did you come from?"
"The bus."
"The bus stop is outside."
"Fast bus."

You walk into the exam at 8:59 and 40 seconds. One sock. Toothbrush in your pocket. Best grade in the class.

[ТАЙМЕР 8:59:40 · ПЛАШКА «1 SOCK»]

## [0:30] QUESTION + PROMISE

Enjoy it. That's the last normal morning you'll ever have.

In one week, you'll eat breakfast in Paris and be home before your parents wake up. In three weeks, a government agent will know your name. And in forty days, you'll make one jump in front of the whole world. A jump you can never take back.

So... what if you could teleport?

[ЗАГОЛОВОК: WHAT IF YOU COULD TELEPORT?]

## [0:50] THE RULES

First, you need to figure out how this thing works. You spend the whole weekend testing in your bedroom. Sam, your best friend, holds a stopwatch and a notebook. Mostly, he eats your chips.

**Rule one.** Big jumps only work to places you've seen with your own eyes. Not photos. Not videos. You stare at a picture of a beach in the Maldives for ten minutes. Close your eyes. Pop. You land in your own shower. Fully dressed. Sam writes it down: "No Maldives."

[ШТАМП: NO MALDIVES]

**Rule two.** Short jumps, under about fifty kilometers, you can do blind. You just face a direction and go. You land roughly where you aimed. Give or take a few hundred meters. Sometimes that's a field. Once, it's a car wash.

**Rule three.** Whatever you're touching comes with you. Your backpack. Your phone. Sam's arm. Sam lands in the kitchen, looks at you, and throws up into the sink. "Next time," he says, "warn me."

[ПЛАШКА: RULE 3B — SAM GETS TELEPORT-SICK]

**Rule four** is the one that almost kills you.

Saturday afternoon, you try your first long jump. Eighty kilometers, to the lake where your grandpa used to fish. You arrive running. Not walking. Running. Like someone shoved you off a moving bike. You stumble three steps and fall face-first into the water.

Here's what happened. And this part is real.

The Earth spins. At the equator, the ground under your feet is moving at about 1,670 kilometers per hour. Faster than the speed of sound. You don't feel it, because everything moves with you. The air, the house, the chips.

[ГЛОБУС, СТРЕЛКИ СКОРОСТИ · 1,670 KM/H · THAT'S REAL]

But every spot on the planet moves at a slightly different speed, in a slightly different direction. When you teleport, you keep the speed of the place you left. So you arrive moving wrong. Eighty kilometers? A shove. Jump straight from your bedroom to Paris, and you'd hit the ground at around 250 kilometers per hour. Like falling out of a race car.

So you learn to hop. Fifty kilometers at a time. Each landing, a small stumble. Let the ground catch you. Then jump again.

Sam does the math on a napkin. "Paris is about a thousand kilometers. That's twenty hops. If you hop every two seconds..."
"Forty seconds."
"Forty seconds," Sam says. "Your knees are gonna hate you."

[СЧЁТЧИК ХОПОВ 0 → 20 · ТАЙМЕР 0:40]

## [3:00] LEVEL 1 — EVERYDAY LIFE

And at first, your life just gets... ridiculously convenient.

Most people spend almost an hour a day commuting. Add that up and it's about ten full days a year, staring at the back of someone else's car. You? Zero. You're never late. Ever.

[ГРАФИК: 10 DAYS / YEAR → 0]

You forget your charger at home. Pop. Got it. Your favorite pizza place is in Naples. Twenty hops. Your pizza is still hot when you get it home. The guy at the counter sees you every Friday at seven. "You live around here?"
"Kind of."

You sleep forty minutes longer every single morning. Your mom thinks you've finally got your life together. You haven't. You've just got a superpower.

## [3:50] LEVEL 2 — THE WORLD

Then you get bolder.

Your first trip to Paris takes twenty blind hops. You land in a cow field. A parking lot. A stranger's wedding, right next to the cake. "Congratulations," you say. Pop.

But once you've stood on that bridge over the river, it's yours forever. Now Paris is one memory away.

[КАРТА: ТОЧКИ ПОСАДОК · ПЛАШКИ «COW», «CAR WASH», «WEDDING»]

Your weekends start to look like this. A croissant in Paris at eight a.m. That's two a.m. back home. Back in bed before anyone notices. You collect cities like stickers. Paris. Rome. Barcelona. Each one is a dot in your head that never fades.

You watch the sunset from a rooftop in Paris. Then you hop home and watch it again an hour later from your own roof. Two sunsets in one evening.

There's just one thing nobody warned you about. Jet lag. Your body has a clock inside it, and it doesn't care how you travel. Pop between time zones every night and your brain stops knowing what day it is. You fall asleep in math class. Twice.
"Late night?" the teacher asks.
"Early morning," you say. "In France."

And here's something wild. Teleportation is technically real. In 2017, Chinese scientists teleported the quantum state of a single photon from the ground to a satellite more than a thousand kilometers away. Not a person. Not even a whole particle. Just its information.

[СПУТНИК, ЛУЧ ФОТОНА · 1,000+ KM · THAT'S REAL]

Scientists teleported one photon. You just teleported a large pepperoni pizza.

## [5:20] THE PRICE

But every superpower has a price. Yours is paperwork.

You've been to eleven countries this month. Your passport has zero stamps.

Then you get careless. You pop onto that Paris bridge at noon, right behind a girl filming her boyfriend's proposal. She doesn't notice you. Her phone does.

By Monday, the clip has fourteen million views. "Hoodie guy appears out of thin air." Slow-motion replays. Red circles. Arrows. Half the comments say it's fake. The other half are trying to figure out who you are.

[СЧЁТЧИК ПРОСМОТРОВ 0 → 14,000,000]

Sam texts you. "Bro. Is that your hoodie?"
"Lots of people have that hoodie."
"It has my ketchup stain on it."

Three weeks after your first jump, there's a knock at the door. A woman in a grey suit. She doesn't smile.

"My name is Agent Hale. You've never left the country. Zero stamps. And yet you were filmed in Paris on Saturday, and forty minutes later you bought a pizza in Naples."

She slides a card across the kitchen table. "Nothing is locked for someone like you. Vaults. Server rooms. Borders. People will pay anything for that. And some of them will do much worse than pay. Work with us. We'll protect you."

And here's the scary part. She's right. Every vault in the world has a door. You don't need doors. You could be a billionaire by Friday. But every dollar you take belongs to somebody. And if you say yes, you're not a person anymore. You're a tool.

"I'll think about it."
"Think fast," she says. "You're not the only one who watched that video."

So now you hide. No more bridges at noon. You jump only from bathrooms and back alleys. You check over your shoulder every time. You can go anywhere on Earth, and suddenly there's nowhere you feel safe.

## [7:25] THE HUMAN STORY

The only place you still go openly is the care home.

Your grandma Rosa lives there, nine hundred kilometers away. Your family used to visit twice a year. Now you visit every Sunday. Eighteen hops. Nobody asks.

Grandma has dementia. Most days, she doesn't remember your name. She calls you "the nice boy." But there's one thing she never forgets. A lemon tree.

She grew up in a tiny village on a cliff above the sea, in the south of Italy. Behind her house stood a lemon tree her father planted the year she was born. She left at seventeen and never went back. Sixty-three years.

"The lemons were as big as your fist," she says. Every single Sunday. "I'd give anything to smell that tree one more time."

The doctors say she's too weak to travel. An airport, a plane, hours in a car. It would be too much. "She doesn't have much time," the nurse tells your mom in the hallway. You hear it anyway.

You could hop blind. But blind hops land you a few hundred meters off. In a village on a cliff, a few hundred meters off means the sea. Not with grandma. With her, every landing has to be perfect. You need to see that tree with your own eyes.

So you do it the slow way. You spend your savings. Two trains, a bus and a ferry. Two days. You climb a hundred steps up a cliff and ask a very confused old man in terrible Italian, "Rosa? Lemon tree? Big lemons?"

He takes you behind an empty house. And there it is. Huge. Ancient. Covered in lemons as big as your fist.

You stand there for ten minutes, memorizing it. Every leaf. The blue sea below. The smell.

Then you go back for her.

Sunday. You tell the nurse you're taking grandma for a walk in the garden. Behind the hedge, you take her hand. "Grandma, close your eyes."

For her, you make the hops tiny. Ten kilometers each. Barely a breath of wind at every landing. A hundred and forty jumps. You hold her hand the whole way. You catch her every time.

[СЧЁТЧИК ХОПОВ 1 → 140 · ДВЕ РУКИ КРУПНЫМ ПЛАНОМ]

And then you're there. Warm sun. Salty air. And the smell of lemons.

She opens her eyes. She doesn't say anything for a long time. She walks up to the tree, slowly, and puts her hand on the trunk, like she's touching an old friend.

"Papà," she whispers.

She picks one lemon and holds it under her nose. She starts crying. Then laughing. Then she turns to you and says, "Nico. My Nico. How did you do this?"

Your name. For the first time in a year, she remembers your name.

You stay for one hour. She tells you stories you've never heard. About her father. About the sea. About the boy she kissed under this tree, who was definitely not grandpa. "Don't tell your mother," she laughs.

Then you hop her home. A hundred and forty jumps. That night, she sleeps with the lemon on her pillow. The nurse can't figure out where it came from.

You can't tell anyone. But for the first time, it feels like this power was made for something.

## [11:00] CLIMAX — THE JUMP YOU CAN'T TAKE BACK

Day forty. You're at home doing homework when the TV turns red. Breaking news.

A storm. The dam above a small valley town has cracked. The river is rising faster than anyone expected. Helicopters can't fly in this wind. Dozens of people are stuck on rooftops, and the water is still coming up.

[ПЛАШКА: BREAKING NEWS · DAM FAILURE · 0 HELICOPTERS]

You know that town. You hop through it every Sunday on the way to grandma.

Sam is on your couch. He looks at the TV. Then at you. "Don't," he says. "There are cameras everywhere. If you go, everyone will know. Hale. Everyone. Forever."

He's right. If you go, there's no more hiding. No more normal life. This is the jump you can never take back.

On the screen, a little girl on a roof is waving a red jacket at a helicopter that can't land.

You look at Sam. "Hold the stopwatch."

Pop.

Freezing rain. The water roaring. The roof is shaking under your feet. You grab the girl and her dad. Pop. A dry hill, a school gym, blankets. Pop. Back. An old man on top of a car. Pop. A woman holding a dog. "Take the dog first!" "I'm taking you both." Pop.

Every jump, your knees hit the ground. Every jump, your head spins. Short hops only. You can't risk the speed. Back and forth. Back and forth.

Sam's voice in your earbuds. "Jump thirty. Jump thirty-one. Bro, you're bleeding."
"I'm fine."
"You are not fine."

[СЧЁТЧИК: JUMPS 31 · PEOPLE 14 · DOGS 1]

On jump forty-seven, the last roof goes under the water. Nobody on it.

Forty-seven jumps. Twenty-three people. Two dogs. One very angry cat.

You lie on the floor of the school gym, soaking wet, staring at the ceiling. Then you hear it. A phone camera beep. Then another. Then fifty. Everyone in that gym is filming you.

By morning, the whole planet knows your face.

## [13:00] RESOLUTION

Two days later, you go to see grandma. There are reporters outside your house, so you hop straight into her room.

The TV is on. It's you. Again. Soaking wet, carrying a little girl in a red jacket. The anchor says, "Who is the teleporting teenager?"

Grandma points at the screen. Then at you. And she smiles. "That's my Nico," she tells the nurse. "He takes me to see lemons."

The nurse laughs and pats her hand. She thinks grandma is confused. Grandma winks at you. She's not confused at all.

That evening, Agent Hale is waiting in your kitchen again. This time she isn't sliding a card across the table. She's holding a newspaper with your face on it.

"You know what you did?" she says. "You made yourself impossible to hide. And impossible to use. Anyone who comes after you now, the whole world will be watching."

Then she does something you've never seen her do. She smiles. "Smart kid."

She leaves. You sit alone in the kitchen. Finally, it's quiet.

## [14:00] CLIFFHANGER

Then the air in front of you ripples.

Pop.

A girl about your age is standing in your kitchen. Grey hoodie. Wet shoes. She looks around like she owns the place.

"So you're the one from the news."
"Who are you?"
She smiles. "Someone who's been teleporting for six years. And trust me. You're doing it all wrong."

Pop. She's gone.

And on the table, right where she was standing, there's a lemon.
